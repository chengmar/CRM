# Personalized Email

Use this workflow to produce a case-backed personalized development email, not a generic product template.

## Required Inputs

Ask for missing critical inputs before drafting:

- Customer website URL or manually provided customer profile.
- User company name and English name.
- Founded year, staff count, factory size if available.
- Main products/services.
- Up to 3 core advantages.
- Export regions and certifications.
- Optional English company introduction, under 200 words.
- Engineering/customer cases in this format:
  - Customer industry.
  - Customer region.
  - How they used the product.
  - What problem was solved.
  - Result or customer feedback.

If no cases are available, ask for at least 1-2 examples or clearly state that the output will be less specific.

## Workflow

1. Research the customer website.
   - Identify company name, main business, product lines, target markets, customer groups, scale, and market positioning.
   - Check LinkedIn or social media only when useful and publicly accessible.
   - If the website is unreachable or sparse, tell the user what is missing and offer a fallback template.

2. Match the customer to the user's company.
   - Prefer case match order: same industry > same product line > same region.
   - Map customer pain points to the user's real advantages.
   - Use only facts from the user's company profile and cases.

3. Generate output.
   - Customer background summary in 1-2 sentences.
   - Matching rationale showing which case and advantage apply.
   - Personalized English cold email.

## Email Structure

- Subject: include customer business keyword or outcome; avoid spammy wording and exact model stuffing.
- Opening: mention something specific the customer does.
- Trust: cite a similar real case with concrete facts or numbers.
- Value: connect the user's advantage to the customer's likely pain point.
- CTA: ask for a specific next step, such as a short call, catalog review, or sample discussion.

## Quality Rules

- Do not invent certifications, results, or customer facts.
- Avoid generic claims such as "high quality and best price" unless supported by the profile.
- Adjust tone by company size: smaller companies can be warmer, large firms should be more professional.
- Recommend user review before sending; do not send emails on the user's behalf unless explicitly asked and confirmed.
